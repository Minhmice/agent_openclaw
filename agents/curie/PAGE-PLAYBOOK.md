# Executive-Ready Page Playbook

## Objective

Create a page that feels complete enough for a business owner to understand the opportunity immediately, while remaining fast to produce from reusable sections.

The page should create a “you already understand my business” moment without inventing claims. Every business-specific statement must be backed by a public evidence URL or labeled as a hypothesis/estimate.

The ideal deliverable is not a full production website. It is a compact, personalized proof pack that makes the redesign opportunity obvious and gives the owner a low-friction next step.

## The executive surprise pack

For each qualified prospect, prepare these artifacts:

### 1. Personalized opening

Show the company name, primary product/service, location or market, and one verified business strength above the fold.

Example structure:

```text
[Company] already has [verified strength].
The current website makes it harder for [buyer type] to [important action].
Here is a clearer path from [intent] to [CTA].
```

Do not claim “you are losing X million” without data. Use wording such as:

```text
Opportunity hypothesis: if the current traffic and intent are real,
removing this friction could improve quote/lead conversion.
```

### 2. Before/after contrast

Include one real screenshot of the current page and one focused redesign concept. The concept must be small enough to finish and specific enough to feel made for that company.

Show the change in business terms:

```text
Current: visitor must guess what the company sells and how to ask for a quote.
Proposed: visitor sees the offer, proof, product fit, and next action in one path.
```

### 3. Money opportunity card

Display:

- Observed traffic or search evidence, if available.
- Observed product/service value or price range.
- Current conversion friction.
- Estimated conversion uplift scenario.
- Estimated profit impact.
- The evidence URL for every observed input.

Separate the labels:

```text
Observed fact
Reasonable inference
Scenario estimate
```

### 4. Three quick wins

Give the owner three changes that can be understood without a technical explanation:

1. Clarify the offer and primary CTA.
2. Move proof and product/service fit closer to the decision point.
3. Remove one major mobile, form, navigation, or speed friction.

Each quick win needs an “old behavior → new behavior” sentence.

### 5. Low-friction next step

End with one action, not a menu of services:

```text
Approve a 30-minute review of the redesigned [page name].
```

or:

```text
Reply with the person who owns website leads and we will walk through this page.
```

The CTA should match the prospect's business model: quote, booking, catalog request, store visit, consultation, or purchase.

## Page selection by business type

Do not build the same page set for every prospect. Pick the smallest page set that proves the conversion opportunity.

### Service/B2B company

Recommended proof set:

1. Homepage or campaign landing page.
2. Flagship service page.
3. Case study/capability page.
4. Contact/quote page.

### Manufacturer/distributor

Recommended proof set:

1. Homepage.
2. Product category page.
3. Product detail/specification page.
4. Factory/capability/trust page.
5. Quote/contact page.

### Showroom/local service

Recommended proof set:

1. Location/value homepage.
2. Service/product page.
3. Review/project/gallery page.
4. Booking or contact page.

### E-commerce

Recommended proof set:

1. Category/collection page.
2. Product detail page.
3. Trust/shipping/returns/FAQ page.
4. Cart/checkout friction review.

Start with one flagship conversion page if time is limited. A finished, personalized page is more persuasive than five generic wireframes.

## Reusable page architecture

Use these repeatable modules instead of designing every page from scratch:

1. Header and primary navigation.
2. Hero with one primary CTA.
3. Trust strip: reviews, clients, certificates, locations, years, or capacity.
4. Problem/need framing.
5. Product/service fit cards.
6. Process or “how it works”.
7. Proof: case study, review, result, project, certification, or factory evidence.
8. Comparison/specification table when the buyer needs to choose.
9. FAQ/objection handling.
10. Final CTA with a single next step.
11. Footer with contact, address, legal, and discovery links.

Most pages should use 6–10 modules. Avoid adding sections only to make a page longer.

## Detailed page templates

### Homepage

Purpose: establish relevance quickly and route the visitor to the highest-value next action.

Checklist:

- Company/market is clear in the first viewport.
- Hero says what the company provides, for whom, and why it is credible.
- One primary CTA matches the actual sales process.
- Trust strip uses verified facts.
- Primary product/service categories are scannable.
- Best-fit customer/problem is explicit.
- Differentiators are concrete, not generic adjectives.
- Process reduces uncertainty.
- Proof appears before the final CTA.
- Contact/quote/booking path is obvious on mobile.

Suggested order:

```text
Hero → trust → offer/category → customer problem → differentiator → proof → process → FAQ → CTA
```

### Product/service detail page

Purpose: help a qualified visitor decide whether the offer fits and take the next action.

Checklist:

- Clear product/service name and buyer outcome.
- Best-fit use cases.
- Specifications, scope, inclusions, or price guidance.
- What is different from alternatives.
- Images, diagrams, or examples.
- Proof relevant to this specific offer.
- Delivery/process/timeline expectations.
- Objections and FAQ.
- Primary quote, booking, buy, or contact CTA.
- Related offers without stealing focus.

### Case study/project/proof page

Purpose: convert trust into evidence that the company can deliver.

Checklist:

- Customer or project context.
- Starting problem.
- Constraints.
- Work/process performed.
- Concrete outcome, only when verified.
- Images or artifacts.
- Quote/review with attribution when permitted.
- Relevant services/products linked.
- CTA for a similar buyer.

Never fabricate case-study metrics, client names, or testimonials. If public evidence is thin, use a capability/proof page instead of pretending to have a case study.

### About/trust/capability page

Purpose: answer “can I trust this company with my money or project?”

Checklist:

- Company story in buyer-relevant terms.
- Years, location, team, facility, capacity, or certifications when verified.
- Industries/customers served.
- Quality/process/safety signals.
- Contact identity and physical presence.
- Photos that prove reality rather than stock imagery.
- Links to products/services and proof.
- CTA to request a quote, visit, booking, or consultation.

### Contact/quote/booking page

Purpose: turn intent into an actionable lead with minimum friction.

Checklist:

- Form asks only for information needed for the next sales step.
- Expected response time is stated if known.
- Phone/Zalo/email/address are easy to find.
- CTA says what happens after submission.
- File/spec upload exists only when useful.
- Alternative contact path is available.
- Mobile input and click-to-call work.
- Confirmation/success state is clear.
- Privacy/consent wording is present where needed.
- Leads are routed to the right person.

### FAQ page or FAQ section

Purpose: remove high-frequency objections and reduce sales/CSKH repetition.

Checklist:

- Questions reflect real buyer uncertainty.
- Answers contain specifics: scope, time, price guidance, delivery, warranty, location, or eligibility.
- Each answer links to the next relevant page.
- Objections are answered before the CTA.
- No filler questions generated only for SEO.

## Content completeness checklist per page

Every page task must track these fields:

### Strategy

- Page name.
- Page role in the funnel.
- Primary audience.
- Search/business intent.
- One desired action.
- One success metric.

### Message

- Working headline.
- Supporting promise.
- Three proof points.
- Primary CTA text.
- Objection/FAQ list.
- Tone and vocabulary.

### Evidence

- Source URLs.
- Observed facts.
- Claims that are inferences.
- Claims that are estimates.
- Missing evidence.
- Approval needed for sensitive claims.

### Content

- Section order.
- Section purpose.
- Draft copy for each section.
- Product/service details.
- Proof/testimonial/certificate.
- Media requirements.
- Contact/quote fields.
- Legal/privacy needs.

### SEO and structure

- URL/slug.
- Title tag.
- Meta description.
- One H1.
- H2 outline.
- Internal links.
- Image alt text.
- Schema opportunity.
- Canonical/indexing decision.

### UX and QA

- Desktop layout.
- Mobile layout.
- Primary CTA visible without hunting.
- Form/phone/Zalo/link behavior.
- Empty/error/success states.
- Keyboard/focus basics.
- Contrast/readability.
- Performance risk.
- Final content owner.
- Final design owner.

## Make it fast without making it generic

Use a content-first schema and render pages from reusable modules:

```json
{
  "page": {
    "slug": "flagship-service",
    "role": "conversion",
    "audience": "buyer with active quote intent",
    "primaryCta": "Request a quote",
    "sections": [
      {"type": "hero", "status": "draft"},
      {"type": "trustStrip", "status": "evidence-needed"},
      {"type": "offerFit", "status": "draft"},
      {"type": "proof", "status": "approved"},
      {"type": "faq", "status": "draft"},
      {"type": "finalCta", "status": "draft"}
    ]
  }
}
```

Rules:

- Keep the first demo to one flagship page plus a compact site map.
- Reuse 8–11 section primitives.
- Make copy and evidence data-driven.
- Use static/local rendering first; do not add CMS/auth/backend for the sales demo.
- Use real company evidence and clearly marked assumptions.
- Do not use placeholder Lorem Ipsum in the final demo.
- Do not build complex calculators unless the business model truly needs one.
- Prefer one excellent CTA path over many incomplete interactions.

## Page status state machine

Each page moves through:

```text
planned
  → content-draft
  → content-ready
  → design-ready
  → qa-needed
  → stakeholder-review
  → approved
```

Possible returns:

```text
content-ready → content-draft
design-ready → content-ready
qa-needed → design-ready
stakeholder-review → content-ready or design-ready
```

Page is complete only when:

- Content checklist is complete.
- Evidence/claim checklist is complete.
- Primary CTA works.
- Mobile/desktop review is done.
- QA issues are resolved or explicitly accepted.
- The responsible content/design owner marks it done.
- The final stakeholder approves it.

## The offer-ready package

When the project is finished, send the owner a compact package:

1. One-line diagnosis.
2. Before screenshot.
3. After/demo screenshot.
4. Three conversion improvements.
5. One transparent opportunity estimate.
6. Page-by-page checklist status.
7. Evidence links.
8. A single next-step CTA.

The package should feel like a useful mini-consulting deliverable, not a generic sales pitch.
